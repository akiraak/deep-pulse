The latest partnerships announced by OpenAI and Anthropic with private equity firms are aimed at speeding up how their AI tools are adopted by businesses.

Anthropic has set up a \$1.5 billion venture backed by investors including Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman Sachs, along with General Atlantic, Apollo Global Management, GIC and Sequoia Capital. The venture includes commitments of about \$300 million each from Anthropic, Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman.

OpenAI is working on a similar but larger structure, reported to be valued at around \$10 billion, and is raising roughly \$4 billion from 19 investors, including TPG, Bain Capital and Brookfield Asset Management. These investors provide access to a large base of companies across sectors.

These partnerships are structured to bring in capital, while also expanding how AI tools are deployed across enterprises linked to private equity firms.

The model is focused on implementation. Anthropic said the new firm brings "additional operating capability to the ecosystem and capital from leading alternative asset managers," and added that "enterprise demand for Claude is significantly outpacing any single delivery model."

To address that demand, the company plans to embed engineers within client organisations. These teams will work with internal staff to identify where AI can be applied, build tools that fit into existing workflows and support them over time. This approach is similar to the model used by Palantir Technologies, where engineers are deployed alongside customers.

Anthropic has described how this could work in sectors such as healthcare. In its example, clinicians spend time on documentation, coding and compliance tasks. Engineers are expected to build systems around these workflows so that such tasks take less time.

OpenAI's planned venture is expected to follow a similar structure, with a focus on helping enterprises integrate its AI tools and services. Reports suggest that both companies are also exploring acquisitions of engineering and consulting firms to expand their ability to deliver these deployments.

The move brings them closer to the work typically handled by IT services and consulting firms. Companies such as Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys, Wipro and HCL Technologies have long focused on implementing technology systems and managing large-scale projects in a market estimated at around \$300 billion.

At the same time, other companies operate in similar or adjacent areas. Consulting firms like Accenture and Capgemini focus on large-scale technology implementation, while software providers including Microsoft and Salesforce are integrating AI features into tools used by enterprises.

Earlier this year, concerns around AI-driven automation contributed to a sharp sell-off in IT stocks, with companies falling 4--7% and the Nifty IT index dropping about 6% in a single session.

At the same time, large enterprises continue to depend on system integration, legacy infrastructure and global delivery capabilities. Anthropic has said it will continue working with systems integrators to deploy its technology for large organisations.

Both companies are also under pressure to scale enterprise use of their tools. OpenAI recently raised \$122 billion in funding at a reported valuation of \$852 billion. Anthropic is said to be targeting a valuation of around \$900 billion in a future round.
