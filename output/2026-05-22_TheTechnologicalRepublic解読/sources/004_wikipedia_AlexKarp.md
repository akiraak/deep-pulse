**Alexander Caedmon Karp** (born October 2, 1967)^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}1[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-CNBC-1)^ is an American businessman and entrepreneur. He is the co-founder and [chief executive officer](/wiki/Chief_executive_officer "Chief executive officer") (CEO) of the software firm [Palantir Technologies](/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"){.mw-redirect}.

Karp earned his [J.D.](/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor") degree from [Stanford Law School](/wiki/Stanford_Law_School "Stanford Law School"), where he met [Peter Thiel](/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"), and a doctorate in [social theory](/wiki/Social_theory "Social theory") from [Goethe University Frankfurt](/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt"). He subsequently worked in finance, founding the investment firm Caedmon Group, where he managed money for clients including wealthy European investors and invested in start-up companies and stocks.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}2[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-inside-2)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)^ Through his connection with Thiel, Karp later helped co-found Palantir alongside three others in 2003.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}2[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-inside-2)^

In 2025, his net worth exceeded \$18 billion, making him among the wealthiest 200 people in the world as reported by *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes "Forbes")* and the *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index "Bloomberg Billionaires Index"), and [Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine named him on the Time 100 list of the world\'s most influential people of that year.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}4[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-time100-2025-4)^

## Early life and background {#Early_life_and_background}

Alexander Caedmon Karp was born in New York City,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}5[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Axel_Springer-5)^ the eldest son of Robert Joseph Karp, a Jewish clinical [pediatrician](/wiki/Pediatrics "Pediatrics"),^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}6[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-6)^ and Leah Jaynes Karp, an [African American](/wiki/African_Americans "African Americans") artist.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}7[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-7)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}8[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:10-8)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}9[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-nytmag-9)^ He grew up with his younger brother, Oliver \"Ben\" Karp, in [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}11[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-11)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}12[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-12)^

Like his father,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}13[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-13)^ Karp attended [Central High School](/wiki/Central_High_School_(Philadelphia) "Central High School (Philadelphia)") in Philadelphia, graduating in 1985.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}8[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:10-8)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}14[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-14)^ He said he struggled with [dyslexia](/wiki/Dyslexia "Dyslexia") from an early age.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}9[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-nytmag-9)^ He was influenced by his parents\' activism for [civil rights](/wiki/Civil_rights_movement "Civil rights movement") and [social justice](/wiki/Social_justice "Social justice") during his youth and went to many protests.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}15[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-15)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)^ He said what he inherited from his maternal side was \"a strong affinity to fighting discrimination\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}16[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-16)^ Karp stated that \"some black people considered me black while some did not\", and said, \"I view me as me. And I\'m very honored to be honored by all groups that will have me.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ Karp\'s recent ancestry on the paternal side of the family is from Germany.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}17[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-17)^ He said that before he went to Germany, he had underestimated how German his upbringing had been.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}18[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:11-18)^

Karp initially wanted to be a [social theorist](/wiki/Social_theory "Social theory").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}19[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-19)^ He earned a bachelor\'s degree in philosophy from [Haverford College](/wiki/Haverford_College "Haverford College") in [Haverford, Pennsylvania](/wiki/Haverford,_Pennsylvania "Haverford, Pennsylvania"), in 1989,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}20[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-20)^ then enrolled at [Stanford Law School](/wiki/Stanford_Law_School "Stanford Law School"), where he earned a [(J.D.)](/wiki/Juris_Doctor "Juris Doctor") in 1992.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}21[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-bizjournals-21)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}22[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Forbes-22)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}5[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Axel_Springer-5)^ While at Stanford, Karp met [Peter Thiel](/wiki/Peter_Thiel "Peter Thiel"). The two bonded over their exasperation with the law school and a passion for political debates. Thiel recalled that, \"He was more the socialist, I was more the capitalist. He was always talking about Marxist theories of alienated labor and how this was true of all the people around us.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}9[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-nytmag-9)^ Karp said that he felt different at \"every institution I interacted with\". At each, he would think, \"I can navigate this place, but I am not a part of it.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)^

After his undergraduate studies and law school, Karp earned a [Ph.D.](/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D."){.mw-redirect} in [neoclassical social theory](/wiki/Classical_social_theory "Classical social theory"){.mw-redirect} from [Goethe University Frankfurt](/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt "Goethe University Frankfurt") in [Germany](/wiki/Germany "Germany") in 2002.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}21[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-bizjournals-21)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}22[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Forbes-22)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}5[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-Axel_Springer-5)^ Thiel was the only one who supported his decision to pursue a doctorate at Frankfurt.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}23[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-DerSpiegel-23)^ His doctoral thesis was titled \"*Aggression in der Lebenswelt: Die Erweiterung des Parsonsschen Konzepts der Aggression durch die Beschreibung des Zusammenhangs von Jargon, Aggression und Kultur*\" (Aggression in the [Lifeworld](/wiki/Lifeworld "Lifeworld"): The Extension of [Parsons](/wiki/Talcott_Parsons "Talcott Parsons")\' Concept of Aggression by Describing the Connection Between Jargon, Aggression, and Culture).^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}24[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-24)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}25[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-25)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}26[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-26)^ Brede told him he could write the thesis in English, but he replied that it was important to him that he could write it in German.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}27[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:12-27)^ He lived in Frankfurt from the mid-1990s to 2001 and considered staying permanently, but ultimately decided to go back to the U.S. for work.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}27[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:12-27)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}18[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:11-18)^

## Career {#Career}

### Business career {#Business_career}

Karp began a career as a research associate at the [Sigmund Freud Institute](/wiki/Sigmund_Freud_Institute "Sigmund Freud Institute") in [Frankfurt](/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}3[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-thetimes-3)^ He received an inheritance of \$12,000 from his paternal grandfather, with which he founded London-based money management firm Caedmon Group.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}28[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger202567-28)^ After he gained some successes with the stock market, some European high-net-worth individuals invested in his company.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}29[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-forbes2013-29)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}28[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger202567-28)^ Karp later claimed that his goal at the time was to amass \$250,000 to return to Berlin to read and write as a dilettante.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}28[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger202567-28)^

For some time, he helped Thiel to gather funding for [Clarium Capital](/wiki/Clarium_Capital "Clarium Capital").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}30[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger202559-30)^ In 2004, along with Thiel and others, Karp co-founded [Palantir Technologies](/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"){.mw-redirect}, becoming its CEO.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}29[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-forbes2013-29)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}31[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:2-31)^ *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* ranked Karp the highest-paid CEO of a [publicly traded](/wiki/Publicly_traded "Publicly traded"){.mw-redirect} company in 2020, the year the company went public, with compensation worth \$1.1[ ]{.nowrap}billion.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}32[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-32)^ In 2024, he was the highest-paid CEO of a publicly traded company in the United States, with a \"compensation actually paid\" of almost \$6.8 billion.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}33[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-nyt-equilar-33)^ *[The Economist](/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")* chose Karp as the 2024 CEO of the Year.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}34[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-34)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}35[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-ceooftheyear-35)^ He is also co-managing director at Frankfurt-based Palantir Technologies Gmbh, and sits on the board of Palantir Technologies UK Ltd..^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}36[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-36)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}37[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-37)^

According to Steinberger, Karp had no previous technical or business training, but his background in philosophy helped with understanding ethical or political concerns.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}38[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger202562-38)^ Karp criticizes companies like Google and Facebook for their attitude to data protection. Facebook became the foil against which Palantir defined its identity; when Palantir expanded in Palo Alto and took over a former Facebook office, they painted over a famous wall associated with Facebook.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}39[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-39)^ Among Palantir leadership, there were differences in philosophy, with Thiel complaining about Karp always choosing the hard, complex way when a more straightforward path might have sufficed. Despite this, Thiel largely left the operation of the company in Karp\'s hands.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}40[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESteinberger2025209-40)^

In 2024, Karp was 1143rd on the *Forbes* annual *World\'s Billionaires List* with a net worth of \$2.9 billion.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}41[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-41)^ In 2025 his net worth at times exceeded \$18 billion, ranking him among the 200 wealthiest people in the world on the *Forbes Real-Time Billionaires List*^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}42[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-42)^ and the *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index "Bloomberg Billionaires Index")*.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}43[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-43)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}44[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-44)^

### Political activities {#Political_activities}

He is a frequent participant of the Reagan National Defense forum, which is an annual gathering of \"generals, military strategists, and defense industrial companies\", now also open to tech leaders like Karp and Bezos.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}48[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-48)^ At the 2023 edition of the forum, he stated that \"Somehow the corporate elite of this country thinks when it's time to make money, you stand up, and when it's time to stand up, you go play golf. And we've got to change that. That's our fault\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}49[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-49)^

The 2025 documentary *Watching You: The World of Palantir and Alex Karp* explores the political and business network that Karp has built in Germany.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}50[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-50)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}51[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:13-51)^ He helps build connections between American and German politicians through transatlantic organizations such as the [Atlantik-Brücke](/wiki/Atlantik-Br%C3%BCcke "Atlantik-Brücke").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}52[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-52)^

He is a member of the steering committee of [Bilderberg Meeting](/wiki/Bilderberg_Meeting "Bilderberg Meeting").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}53[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-53)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}54[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-54)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}55[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-55)^ The secret meeting between Karp, Thiel and Sweden\'s prime minister [Ulf Kristersson](/wiki/Ulf_Kristersson "Ulf Kristersson") at the [2025 Bilderberg meeting](/wiki/2025_Bilderberg_Conference "2025 Bilderberg Conference") has caused controversies in Sweden (Kristersson was not on the official list).^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}56[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-56)^ Karp also participates in the [Munich Security Conference](/wiki/Munich_Security_Conference "Munich Security Conference").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}57[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-57)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}58[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-58)^

Dutch MEP [Sophie in 't Veld](/wiki/Sophie_in_%E2%80%99t_Veld "Sophie in ’t Veld"){.mw-redirect} tracks the close proximity between Karp and his company Palantir with EU leaders like [Ursula von der Leyen](/wiki/Ursula_von_der_Leyen "Ursula von der Leyen") and [Margrethe Vestager](/wiki/Margrethe_Vestager "Margrethe Vestager"), noting that when the EU delegation visited Washington in 2019, the only private company they contacted was Palantir.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}59[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-59)^ In June 2022, he was the first CEO of a major Western company to meet president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy "Volodymyr Zelenskyy") after [Russia invaded Ukraine](/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present) "Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}60[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-60)^

## Views {#Views}

### Political views {#Political_views}

#### On the West {#On_the_West}

In 2025 *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* magazine listed Karp as one of [the world\'s 100 most influential people](/wiki/Time_100 "Time 100"), calling him \"the embodiment of a new kind of Silicon Valley billionaire: an unashamed techno-nationalist who evangelizes Western power\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}4[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-time100-2025-4)^ In naming him to the Time 100 list, the magazine noted that Karp had once quoted [Samuel P. Huntington](/wiki/Samuel_P._Huntington "Samuel P. Huntington")\'s *The Clash of Civilizations* in a letter he wrote to investors: \"The rise of the West was not made possible \'by the superiority of its ideas or values or religion \... but rather by its superiority in applying organized violence. \[\...\] Westerners often forget this fact; non-Westerners never do.\'\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}4[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-time100-2025-4)^ During a *New York Times* interview, Karp said that \"you scare the crap out of your adversaries\", asking, \"Are we tough enough to scare our adversaries so we don't go to war? Do the Chinese, Russians and Persians think we're strong?\" [Maureen Dowd](/wiki/Maureen_Dowd "Maureen Dowd"), a journalist, said the interview was \"brim\[ming\] with American [chauvinism](/wiki/Chauvinism "Chauvinism")\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^

In 2024 *The New York Times* again quoted Karp, who said that he and his company, Palantir, had \"a consistently pro-Western view, that the West has a superior way of living and organizing itself\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ In the same interview, Karp said that the US would very likely face a three-front war against China, Russia, and Iran in the future. He opined that the best way to prepare for such a war would be developing autonomous weapons. In Karp\'s opinion, the West and the China-Russia-Iran axis were at technological parity. However, he added, the West had a disadvantage when it came to moral disparity because the West would likely not use nuclear weapons, although their adversaries might. He also told the *Times* that he favored military conscription.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^

#### On US politics {#On_US_politics}

In 2018, Karp said he is a socialist^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}61[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-61)^ and a progressive (\"but not woke\").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ In addition he said he voted for [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton "Hillary Clinton").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}62[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:1-62)^ In 2024, the *[Financial Times](/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")* identified Karp as \"a major Biden donor\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}63[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-63)^ [*Current Affairs*](/wiki/Current_Affairs_(magazine) "Current Affairs (magazine)") editor [Nathan J. Robinson](/wiki/Nathan_J._Robinson "Nathan J. Robinson") wrote in 2024 that Karp \"seems to have some idiosyncratic personal definition in mind that has nothing in common with the [socialist tradition](/wiki/Types_of_socialism "Types of socialism")\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}64[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-64)^ In 2024, Karp said that while he was \"not thrilled\" with the direction of the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)"), he would still be \"voting against [Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump")\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}65[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-65)^ That same year, he called for the Democrats to project more strength. He has also protested open-border immigration policies in the U.S. and Europe: \"You have an open border, you get the far right. \[\...\] And once you get them, you can\'t get rid of them.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ In 2025, he said that he views himself as a \"[classic \[*sic*\] liberal](/wiki/Classical_liberalism "Classical liberalism")\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}66[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-66)\[*[[non-primary\ source\ needed]{title="This claim needs references to reliable secondary sources. (January 2026)"}](/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")*\]^

In a November 2025 interview with *[Wired](/wiki/Wired_(magazine) "Wired (magazine)")*, he said that to a family member that disagreed with him in a private conversation, \"I would be pointing out that Trump's decisions on AI, and his decisions on the Middle East, are very different than people in the Democratic Party would have made, and very good.\" Also, he would consider the Democratic Party to have left him if the [Zohran Mamdani](/wiki/Zohran_Mamdani "Zohran Mamdani") wing took over, which he viewed as the result of \"\[the\] role played \[by\] universities and elite institutions \[that are\] teaching pagan religion views\", describing one \"\[portraying an\] AI-driven, AGI environment where no one has a job \[because\] labor is going to be valueless\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}67[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-wired-67)^

Interviewed by his biographer [Michael Steinberger](/wiki/Michael_Steinberger "Michael Steinberger") (whose book *The Philosopher in the Valley: Alex Karp, Palantir, and the Rise of the Surveillance State* was published in November 2025), Karp cites the Democrats\' unresponsiveness to immigration, Iran and attitude to antisemitism (\"They talk all the time about racism but won\'t talk about antisemitism\") as reasons for his disillusion with the party. Steinberger remarks that Karp was particularly concerned about the anti-Israel protests that erupted after the October 7 attacks in Israel.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}68[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-68)^

Karp has condemned \"[woke](/wiki/Woke "Woke")\" ways of thinking, calling them the central risk to his company Palantir and to the United States as a whole.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}69[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:5-69)^ He has called Palantir a \"counter-example\" to companies he considers \"woke\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}69[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:5-69)^ In 2025, he adopted the framing [woke left](/wiki/Woke_left "Woke left"){.mw-redirect} and [woke right](/wiki/Woke_right "Woke right"){.mw-redirect}, defining the former as the aforementioned Mamdani wing and the latter as \"everything is a conspiracy, any use of technology is actually going to only be used to eviscerate and attack us\", categorizing both as \"\[opposed to\] meritocracy\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}67[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-wired-67)^

#### On the United States government {#On_the_United_States_government}

See also: [Palantir Technologies § U.S. military, intelligence, and police](/wiki/Palantir_Technologies#U.S._military,_intelligence,_and_police "Palantir Technologies"){.mw-redirect}

Karp said that technology companies like Palantir have an obligation to support the U.S. military.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}62[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:1-62)^ He said that he and Palantir are \"active in defending the values of the [West](/wiki/Western_world "Western world")\" and \"our belief that the West is a superior way to live\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}69[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:5-69)^ He defended Palantir\'s contract with [U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") (ICE) during the controversy over [family separations](/wiki/Trump_administration_family_separation_policy "Trump administration family separation policy"), saying that while separations are \"a really tough, complex, jarring moral issue\", he favors \"a fair but rigorous immigration policy\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}70[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-70)^ He said the U.S. government should have a strong hand in tech regulation^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}71[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-71)^ and that western countries should dominate AI research.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}72[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-72)^

#### On the Gaza war {#On_the_Gaza_war}

See also: [Palantir Technologies § Israel](/wiki/Palantir_Technologies#Israel "Palantir Technologies"){.mw-redirect}

Karp made a number of remarks on the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war "Gaza war") strongly supporting Israel. He has strongly condemned the [2024 pro-Palestinian protests on university campuses](/wiki/2024_pro-Palestinian_protests_on_university_campuses "2024 pro-Palestinian protests on university campuses"){.mw-redirect}, calling their views a \"pagan religion\" and \"an infection inside of our society\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}73[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:3-73)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}74[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:6-74)^ At the AI Expo for National Competitiveness, he remarked that \"the peace activists are war activists\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}73[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:3-73)^ and said that protestors should be sent to [North Korea](/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}74[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:6-74)^ In December 2023 during the demonstrations at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in [Manhattan, New York](/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan"), he said \"There is literally no way to explain the investment in our elite schools, and the output is a pagan religion---a pagan religion of mediocrity, and discrimination, and intolerance, and violence.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}75[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:7-75)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ Palantir announced that they would set aside 180 positions for Jewish college graduates, citing alleged antisemitism on college campuses related to the protests.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}69[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:5-69)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}75[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:7-75)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^

#### On Germany {#On_Germany}

When Karp was a PhD student at Frankfurt, he criticized [Martin Walser](/wiki/Martin_Walser "Martin Walser")\'s opposition to Germany\'s culture of Holocaust remembrance. (Walser had argued that it was a moral cudgel that the liberal intelligentsia used to suppress nationalism in the newly united Germany.) Decades later, Karp and Nicholas Zamiska published *The Technological Republic*, which embraces Walser\'s idea, saying that it represented \"the forbidden desires and feelings of a nation\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}76[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-76)^ Professor Karola Brede, who served as referee for Karp's doctoral dissertation,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}77[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-77)^ notes that Karp always had a type of political agility associated with liberalism. When Karp was young, he had an affinity with the [FDP](/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_(Germany) "Free Democratic Party (Germany)"){.mw-redirect}. He tried to persuade [CDU](/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_of_Germany "Christian Democratic Union of Germany") member [Michel Friedman](/wiki/Michel_Friedman "Michel Friedman"), who lived on the same street in Frankfurt, that Friedman had chosen the wrong party.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}27[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:12-27)^ In 2019, however, Karp said that in Germany he would vote for the CDU.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}78[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-78)^

In September 2025, Karp called on German leaders to \"talk about the crisis\". He suggested a plan to improve the situation in Germany and save the industry: (1) promoting the political power of the founders and business leaders, who would bring about the change, rather than promoting the power of the political class. He specifically referred to [SAP](/wiki/SAP "SAP") CEO [Christian Klein](/wiki/Christian_Klein "Christian Klein"); (2) creating special technological zones with fewer regulations and laxer labor rules; (3) creating specialized micromodels of economic structures (instead of copying Silicon Valley) that focused on Germany\'s strengths in industrial AI for cars, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and mechanical engineering; (4) informing the population about a looming economic crisis, explaining that both prosperity and the welfare state could only be maintained if industrial and technological development could be secured, while noting that German pacifism was the country\'s Achilles\' heel and should be dealt with when the country has created sufficient deterrents; and (5) countering misguided policies on immigration, energy (especially nuclear energy policy) and technology, all of which had squandered the German advantage and should be changed. He saw his company Palantir as a continuation of Germany\'s cultural legacy and virtues. He also complained about the public\'s perception of his friend and co-founder Peter Thiel, who he said was undeservingly vilified by the media.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}79[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-79)^

At the 2023 opening of *Dimensions: Digital Art since 1859*, an exhibit in Leipzig supported and sponsored by Karp, former German vice chancellor [Sigmar Gabriel](/wiki/Sigmar_Gabriel "Sigmar Gabriel"), and CEO [Tim Höttges](/wiki/Tim_H%C3%B6ttges "Tim Höttges"){.mw-redirect} of [Deutsche Telekom](/wiki/Deutsche_Telekom "Deutsche Telekom") (an advocate for more creative control in the U.S. but a more innovative spirit in Europe), Karp stated: \"We can\'t leave the entire future to the Americans.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}80[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-80)^

In 2026, Palantir summarized one of the arguments in Karp and Zamiska\'s *Technological Republic* as \"The postwar neutering of Germany and Japan must be undone\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}81[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-81)^

### Business views {#Business_views}

Karp is a critic of [short sellers](/wiki/Short_sell "Short sell"){.mw-redirect}, and said he loves \"burning the short sellers\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}82[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:4-82)^ He compared them to cocaine addicts and said that they \"just love pulling down great American companies\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}82[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:4-82)^ In 2024, he received criticism for selling \$1.9 billion of Palantir shares.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}83[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:9-83)^ In February 2025, during a talk promoting his book, he said, \"I love the idea of getting a drone and having light fentanyl-laced urine spraying on analysts that tried to screw us.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}83[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:9-83)^

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Karp lives in [Lyman](/wiki/Lyman,_New_Hampshire "Lyman, New Hampshire"), [New Hampshire](/wiki/New_Hampshire "New Hampshire")^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}84[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-billionaires-84)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}85[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-85)^ and owns 11 houses around the world.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}86[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:0-86)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}87[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-87)^ In a 2024 interview he described himself as Jewish,^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ commenting (after being asked) in another interview that while his background informed Palantir having Israel as a client, he did not believe it expressly did so.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}67[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-wired-67)^

As of 2025, Karp is in long-term relationships with two women. He was described as \"geographically monogamous\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}88[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-88)^

Karp never learned to drive a car. He said, \"I was too poor. And then I was too rich."^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)^ He has said that the thought of having children \"gives \[him\] hives\".^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}2[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-inside-2)^ His uncle, Gerald Jaynes, is the [A. Whitney Griswold](/wiki/Alfred_Whitney_Griswold "Alfred Whitney Griswold") Professor of Economics, African American Studies, and Urban Studies at [Yale](/wiki/Yale_University "Yale University").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}10[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:8-10)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}89[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-89)^

Karp is fluent in [German](/wiki/German_language "German language") and speaks [French](/wiki/French_language "French language").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}2[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-inside-2)^ According to Michael Steinberger, \"No matter where \[Karp\] is, he still speaks German for half the day. He has Austrian assistants who are also his closest confidants --- two or three of them are almost always with him. In every Palantir branch, he has his own office, and when he\'s there, German is often spoken.\"^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}23[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-DerSpiegel-23)^

In 2025, he received the Lamplighter award, presented to him by Rabbi [Levi Shemtov](/wiki/Levi_Shemtov "Levi Shemtov").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}90[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-90)^

## In media {#In_media}

Karp is the subject of the 2024 [German](/wiki/Cinema_of_Germany "Cinema of Germany") documentary film *Watching You: The World of Palantir and Alex Karp*, directed by [Klaus Stern](/w/index.php?title=Klaus_Stern&action=edit&redlink=1 "Klaus Stern (page does not exist)"){.new}[ \[[de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Stern_(Dokumentarfilmer) "de:Klaus Stern (Dokumentarfilmer)"){.extiw}\]]{.noprint style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;"}, which explores Palantir\'s influence and Karp\'s career, including interviews with former colleagues, politicians, and critics; Karp chose not to participate in the documentary.^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}51[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-:13-51)^

Karp, with co-author with Nicholas Zamiska, published *The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West* in February 2025. The book offers a critical perspective on Silicon Valley\'s complacency and the West\'s waning ambition, arguing that the software industry must partner with government to tackle urgent challenges, particularly the [AI arms race](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_arms_race "Artificial intelligence arms race").^[[\[]{.cite-bracket}91[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-91)[[\[]{.cite-bracket}92[\]]{.cite-bracket}](#cite_note-92)^
